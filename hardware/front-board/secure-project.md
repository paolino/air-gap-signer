# Air Gap Signer · secure project definition

The owner leaves the microSD installed, recognizes the enrolled signing board on an independent device before entering a PIN, and approves a specific Cardano signing request using physical controls. A recovery copy of the Pi image works. Recovering a wallet on a second signing board still gives the owner a new device to enroll.

This document defines the engineering target. The direct-GPIO Pi 3B schematic and running prototype are bench references. The production part selection and security claims require the evidence below. Local approval assumes the display, its connection and the buttons remain physically trustworthy. The phone supplies a possession factor. Transaction review and key management take place on the signer.

## What counts as the signer

| Component | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Signing front board | A controller boots authenticated firmware, enforces an update version floor, holds a per-device attestation identity and exclusively controls the display, backlight, physical buttons and PIN entry. The identity is distinct from wallet keys and bound to this controller. STM32H573 with Secure Manager is the first evaluation candidate. |
| Secure element | A soldered SE051C2 holds the wallet signing keys. Require a per-unit authenticated, encrypted controller–chip channel and chip-enforced key-use policy with PIN retries. Pair the controller and chip during provisioning. Loss or replacement of either requires explicit recovery. |
| Raspberry Pi, internal microSD and USB stick | The USB stick carries transactions. The Pi decodes incoming camera QR frames and forwards bounded messages to the controller. Treat every byte the Pi supplies as untrusted. Through the plug-on header the Pi supplies power and exchanges bounded messages. It has no electrical route to the PIN buttons, display bus or secure-element bus. A host may interrupt power or withhold data, causing the session to abort. |
| Paired phone | An offline second factor with its own authentication key. It authenticates requests from the enrolled board and releases a single-use response on an explicit owner action. It can show the board name and operation type. Transaction decoding, wallet keys and the signer PIN stay on the signer. |
| Enclosure | Larger 3.5-inch landscape display, tactile navigation, distinct Confirm and Mode, plug-on assembly, feet and clearance for exposed ports and their plug bodies. Provide service access to the internal microSD after power-off. A camera mounted to the front panel above the display reads QR codes displayed by the paired phone. |

“Same signer” means the enrolled controller operating with its paired secure element. Replacing the Pi or making an exact copy of its card is an allowed service action. A changed host image may cause an error or loss of service. The security requirement is that it cannot collect the PIN, supply trusted review text or cause a signature. This design provides no inventory of host or SD substitutions.

## What happens in the owner’s hands

1. **Enrollment.** During trusted initial setup, verify the board identity and approved build by a trusted provisioning record or independently authenticated enrollment procedure. Pin that specific board key on the phone and enroll the phone’s authentication public key on the board. An unknown board presented during routine use cannot enroll itself. Install authenticated policy updates on the offline verifier through an explicit update flow.
2. **Recognition.** At the start of a signing session the phone creates a fresh random challenge. The phone displays the challenge as a QR code for the signer camera. The board displays its signed evidence for the phone camera. The phone verifies the signature against the enrolled key, the outstanding single-use challenge, the production lifecycle and approved measurements covering all code that controls review, PIN and signing. Missing evidence or an outdated policy outside its validity gives no positive result. The phone reports “Enrolled controller recognized.” The owner proceeds only if they also trust the physical interface. A reset or interrupted session requires fresh recognition and PIN authorization.
3. **Review.** The controller snapshots one request, decodes its supported Cardano transaction body, computes the signing digest from the original body bytes and derives review fields from that same snapshot. Show the signing key, operation, outputs including assets, fees and applicable transaction conditions. Identify claims that would require independent ledger context. Refuse unsupported semantics. An approved decoder and signing policy ship in authenticated firmware or an authenticated release component. A USB-provided label, byte range, decoder or digest has no authority to select what is signed.
4. **Confirm.** Apply the review policy and distinguish fields the owner explicitly waived from fields actually presented. After successful presentation a fresh physical Confirm press selects the reviewed operation. The controller requests a second-factor response bound to the enrolled signer, operation type, key identity, digest of the immutable request, session and a fresh challenge. Only an authenticated request from the board can trigger the phone prompt. The signer displays its authenticated request as a QR code. After the owner taps Allow, the phone displays its signed response for the signer camera. The transaction body stays on the signer side of this exchange. The controller checks the PIN authorization and the paired phone’s response and consumes the pending approval once before invoking the chip. Any change of request or key, display failure, cancellation or reset invalidates pending approval. A retried transport request cannot silently trigger another signing operation. Only the resulting public data and signature may return through the Pi.
5. **Recovery.** A replacement SD can be imaged and installed without changing enrollment. Wallet backup and restore require an authenticated owner operation and a trusted path that keeps plaintext key material off the host, card and transport. Specify and test each supported key format before claiming compatibility. Confirm the restored public key and test signature with an independent implementation. Restoring the wallet on a new board requires explicit new-device enrollment. Loss of the verifier requires trusted re-enrollment before normal use resumes.

### Optical transport

The Pi and its camera may supply arbitrary input. The controller accepts only the bounded message type expected in the current phase, checks its session and verifies any required signature before changing authorization state. Partial scans, duplicate frames and cancelled sessions cannot authorize signing. Use the trusted display for outgoing codes. Transaction import and signature export continue over the USB stick. Camera images and QR content have no direct path to wallet-key operations.

## On-device key management

The integrated 3.5-inch display and physical controls are required for managing keys, and for reviewing transactions. The controller builds the key list from its paired secure element. For each occupied slot, show the slot and a fingerprint derived from the public key, with the full public key available for comparison. The owner can distinguish keys even if labels match.

When creating a key, identify the empty destination slot and show the resulting public-key fingerprint. During restore, identify the destination and let the owner compare the resulting public key against their recovery record. Before deletion, name the operation, show the selected slot and fingerprint, and explain that the key will be removed from this signer. Require a deliberate local confirmation tied to that selection. Cancel or a change of selection clears the pending operation. Listing public keys and comparing fingerprints are local actions. Creating, restoring, deleting or backing up a wallet key requires local confirmation and a phone response bound to that key operation during normal use. Lost-factor recovery follows the separate recovery procedure.

## What the owner actually sees

A malicious replacement panel can show the recipient the owner expects while the genuine controller is asking for approval of a payment to another recipient. The owner can press the genuine Confirm button. Boot verification, controller recognition and the secure element can all succeed in this scenario, even with no live relay. A successful panel transfer only establishes that the controller sent the frame.

The phone authenticates participation in this exact operation. The signer screen is the owner’s only transaction display. If a substituted panel misrepresents the recipient and the owner supplies the factor, the genuine controller can sign a payment the owner did not intend.

## Captured PIN followed by theft

Assume someone learns the correct PIN and later takes the genuine signer while the owner keeps the paired phone. A standalone PIN-and-button design lets that person authorize a payment. Chip retry limits protect against guessing, and device recognition on the owner's phone authenticates the board to the owner. Neither control requires the thief to possess the phone.

The signer requires a fresh response from the separately held phone for each signing request. The owner reviews the transaction on the signer and supplies the possession factor on the phone. The board must enforce this for every route to the wallet key. Replacing the paired credential or exporting a usable backup requires the existing credential or a separately held recovery credential. A factory reset that permits new enrollment must first erase access to the old wallet. The security assumption is that the owner retains the phone and recovery credential.

### Where the phone factor is enforced

The controller flow above enforces the phone response in firmware. Independent enforcement inside the SE051C2 is a hardware evaluation target. NXP documents signing permissions scoped to an authentication object, authenticated sessions and secure messaging. A candidate is to reserve signing permission for a phone-authenticated session and have the phone authenticate only the command for the locally confirmed key and digest. Session credentials must remain outside the Pi and controller. Verify how this composes with the local PIN and controller binding on the installed applet. Provisioning two separate grants of signing permission would allow either grant to authorize signing.

Record the applet version and use a disposable key. Observe the chip’s own refusal with the correct PIN and controller credential but no phone. Observe one valid signature for A with the phone, then refusal of a changed digest, a different key and a repeated command. Also check that the phone alone cannot sign. A controller-side rejection only demonstrates firmware enforcement. ## The live-forwarding decision

Consider two identical-looking boxes. A standalone substitute cannot produce fresh evidence for the enrolled key. A substitute with a live link to the genuine board could forward the phone’s challenge and return the genuine answer. The phone would accurately identify the participant in the exchange while the owner could still enter their PIN on the substitute. Neither a fresh challenge nor a QR establishes physical proximity.

The first architecture can be built and evaluated for standalone substitution, replay and hostile host media. A broad claim that the owner can trust any enclosure put in front of them remains withheld. Before approving field use, decide whether the expected custody conditions allow live forwarding to be excluded. The phone’s second-factor response does not establish that the visible panel belongs to the enrolled controller. Detecting the physical enclosure or proving proximity would be a further, separate requirement. Initial enrollment, the verifier itself and invasive modification of the trusted front board are explicit trust assumptions.

## Evidence required for release

| Test | Observable result |
|---|---|
| Board A and board B have the same release and wallet | Phone accepts enrolled A with a fresh challenge and reports B as a different device before PIN entry. A captured response, missing measurement or unapproved firmware receives no positive result. |
| Authenticated update, rollback, interruption, recovery | A signed and approved update retains board identity. A modified build or a version below the installed minimum cannot authorize signing. The verifier rejects releases revoked in its authenticated policy. An interrupted update either boots an approved version or enters authenticated recovery. Restoring an older card cannot reset firmware or verifier policy floors. |
| Replace SD, replace host, exchange secure elements | A spare SD works. An untrusted host cannot read controls, draw a review frame or transact directly with the chip. An unpaired chip cannot be used for signing. Check the production netlist and the physical assembly. |
| Review Cardano fixtures and change a request during review | An independent Cardano implementation verifies the successful signature for the exact reviewed body and key. A mismatched selection, unsupported feature, malformed input, cancelled session or stale approval produces zero signing calls. |
| Exercise the phone factor with disposable keys | The owner reviews on the signer. A local Confirm alone and a phone response alone each produce zero signing calls. A valid response succeeds once for its approved operation. Changing the signer, key, operation, request or session invalidates it. |
| Exchange codes with the paired phone | Complete recognition and one disposable-key approval with the transaction stick left in place. An unreadable or incomplete scan, a wrong-phase message, a different phone or a replay yields zero signatures. Check that a cancellation followed by a new request discards earlier frames. |
| Observe display and input on the assembled board | With a failed panel transfer or a Confirm press begun before the frame is ready, the board issues zero signing calls. Check the physical panel and input timing on the selected display. |
| Manage two disposable keys with identical labels | Distinguish both by their device-derived public keys. Cancel a deletion and verify both keys remain. Confirm an authorized deletion and verify only the displayed key is removed. After restore, compare the restored public key with the recovery record on the signer screen. |
| Exercise PIN, secure channel and recovery with disposable keys | The chip refuses unauthorized signing and deletion, persists the retry limit through power cycling, and refuses an unpaired host. Loss of the secure channel aborts key loading. Restore reproduces the wallet key and preserves the board identity rules. |
| Captured PIN and stolen genuine signer, with phone and recovery credential withheld | For the phone-authorized design, zero signatures, including after reboot and attempted reuse of an earlier approval. PIN alone cannot replace the paired credential or obtain a usable wallet backup. A deliberate wipe may start a new wallet only. |
| Try an exchange forwarded to enrolled A | Record precisely what the verifier accepts. Evaluate that result against the field-use decision above. |

**First milestone.** Use two development boards, one offline verifier and disposable credentials. Demonstrate A versus B, stale evidence and measured review-firmware changes with the same wallet public identity. On the H573, install the test application using the measured boot/update path. ST documents that an application loaded directly from the IDE can lack its application measurement. Record the build hashes, lifecycle, accepted measurements and verifier outcomes. Bench success establishes recognition under the specified conditions. PCB routing follows review of bus ownership, secure-element provisioning and the controller’s ability to decode and display the supported transaction set.
